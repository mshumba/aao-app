# Architecture and catalog

## Navigation and presentation

`src/app/` is an Expo Router root stack whose first screen is the `(tabs)` group: **Home** `/`, **Paths** `/paths`, **Search** `/search` (built in `src/search/`; an initial `q` is searched at once, and an old `?category=` link opens that topic's page, since Search no longer filters by topic) and **Account** `/account` (the signed-in profile or a guest card, settings, policies and Delete account). Topics is no longer a tab: `/topics` redirects to Search, whose empty screen lists **Browse by topic** for topics with at least 6 sermons (`MIN_TOPIC` in `src/search/parts.tsx`; every topic in the bundled sample), so with today's four demo assignments it is empty for the full library. Topic pages also open from a sermon's topic tags and Home's **From your topics** rail. `src/tabBar.tsx` draws the floating tab pill; switching tabs doesn't animate. Topic pages `/topics/[categoryId]`, sermons `/sermon/[id]`, paths and teaching series `/path/[id]` (the days as a row of cards that opens on today's, with the next day at its edge and the rest a swipe away), series `/series/[key]`, all sermons `/sermons`, `/saved`, the account settings under `/account/*`, About, Privacy, Terms and Sample screens open over the tabs with a Back (or Close) button. There are no download or watch-history controls.

Onboarding and sign-in routes stack over whatever the viewer was doing and close together when an account is ready: `/welcome` and `/onboarding/*` (first run), the sign-up sheet `/join` (an iOS form sheet), and `/auth/email`, `/auth/password`, `/auth/code`, `/auth/reset`, `/auth/new-password` (modals) and `/auth/callback` (where web and OAuth sign-ins return). `/s/[id]` is a shared link: it lets a guest watch that sermon, then opens `/sermon/[id]`. The root layout's provider order is `MotionProvider` → `CatalogueProvider` → `AccountProvider` → `LearningProvider` (needs, paths, moments and path progress) → `PlayerProvider` → `ToastProvider`; `IntentRunner` and `FirstRun` sit beside the player host.

Each tab keeps its own state (typed search, scroll) while you switch. Screens opened over the tabs return with Back; recovery actions ("Browse other topics", "Back to sermons", "Continue as guest") use `exitTo` in `src/navigation.ts`, which returns to the existing tab instead of stacking another (`router.dismissTo` on native; on web, close then switch tab, so browser history keeps one entry per place). The root stack is anchored on `(tabs)`, so a link straight to a sermon or topic opens over Home. Unknown category/sermon links have explicit recovery actions. Route params are normalised (`firstParam`), so a repeated parameter can't break a screen. On web, URL parameters seed the screen on arrival; subsequent typing/filter changes aren't synchronised back into the URL.

`src/theme.ts` holds colour, spacing, radius, type-role and press tokens. The colours are the ivory-and-navy palette (user decision, 14 October 2026): an ivory page (`c.bg`), white raised surfaces lifted by a hairline (`c.border`) or a soft shadow, sand for featured cards (`c.feature`), one navy accent for actions, links, selection and focus (`c.accent`, with `c.onAccent` on it) and gold for editorial eyebrows (`c.gold`). The player and anything drawn over video or artwork use `c.media`, `c.onMedia` and `c.onMediaMuted`, so video stays dark on the light app. `src/ui.tsx` provides shared controls (`Tap` press feedback, buttons, headings with real levels, `SermonTitle`, `SermonRow`, state panels). `src/toast.tsx` shows brief, announced confirmations above the tab bar and dock; `src/share.ts` shares a sermon's link. `src/topics.tsx` owns the topic grid, rows, tags, search filter and the sample-topic notice. `src/navigation.ts` drops repeated taps: each control is locked after it navigates, and an app-wide 350ms window stops a double tap on Back from popping two screens. `src/motion.tsx` keeps the system Reduce Motion setting live for the stack (platform push, or fade) and press feedback (scale, or dim). Side-by-side layouts stack when the reading column is narrow at the user's text size. Text containers grow and screens scroll.

## Catalog model

The library is every long-form video on the [Apostle Arome Osayi YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@ApostleAromeOsayi) (about 2,800, including sermons by other ministers shared there; user decision, 14 October 2026), served from Supabase so new sermons appear without an app update.

- **Supabase** holds `public.sermons` (keyed by `youtube_id`, the stable key for saved sermons, watch progress and later transcript embeddings) and `public.series`, both readable by everyone but not writable by anonymous or signed-in app clients ([migration](../../supabase/migrations/20261014200000_sermon_catalogue.sql)). Server-side importers use privileged access. `scripts/import-catalogue.mts` loads them from the channel manifest; see [Supabase setup](../../supabase/README.md#7-load-the-sermon-catalogue).
- **Nightly discovery** uses the approved channel `UCh6Sn6v_mdhwWTEpNvo5A4A` and public YouTube metadata to insert missing catalogue rows without changing existing editorial data. It defaults to dry-run, excludes clips of 180 seconds or less and reviewed IDs in `src/catalogue/importExclusions.ts`, and caps ordinary applies at 25 candidates. Both importers share normalization in `scripts/catalogue-rows.ts`. Its main-only `catalogue-import` Environment and explicit schedule opt-in are described in [nightly sermon discovery](nightly-sermons.md). This job does not ingest transcripts, build semantic embeddings, or create approved moments, blessings or path days.
- **Titles** are read by `src/catalogue/parseTitle.ts` (shared by the import and the tests): a short headline, the event, the series and part or day, which part of the event ("Day 2 Evening", "Grand Finale"), named guest ministers, the venue and the preaching date, which often differs from the upload date. The exact YouTube title is stored untouched and shown under "On YouTube". `src/catalogue/editorial.ts` holds hand-written headlines that win over parsed ones.
- **On the device**, `src/catalogue/CatalogueProvider.tsx` (`useCatalogue()`) shows the copy saved in AsyncStorage at once, then asks Supabase only for rows changed since (`updated_at`), and fetches everything again weekly so removals leave. The sync logic (`src/catalogue/sync.ts`, `records.ts`) is pure and unit-tested; `remote.ts` reads the REST API with the public key. With no saved copy and no network, the four bundled sermons in `src/data/sermons.ts` stand in and Home says it's offline.
- **On-device account mode** (`EXPO_PUBLIC_AUTH_MODE=local`, used by the automated tests) and builds without a Supabase project use only the bundled four, so tests are deterministic. Series rails on Home appear only with the full library.

`src/data/sermons.ts` turns records into the `Sermon` the screens use (headline, event line parts, display date and duration) and has the list helpers: `searchSermons` (every query word across headline, exact title, event, session, speaker, venue and the date in several spellings, plus sample topics and tags), `sermonsInCategory`, `seriesSermons`, `seriesRails` and `relatedSermons`. Lists are newest first by the day preached, else uploaded. `src/data/categories.ts` defines stable editorial IDs. Topic assignments live separately in `src/data/edification.ts`, keyed by sermon ID, so a sermon is stored once even when it belongs to several topics:

```ts
H9sRbSUt0YE: {
  primaryCategoryId: 'spiritual-growth',
  additionalCategoryIds: ['faith'],
  tags: ['discipleship', 'maturity'],
  reviewStatus: 'demo',
}
```

A sermon's topic labels (primary first, or the topic being browsed first) appear above each row's title. Category membership includes primary and secondary IDs. `searchSermons` matches every whitespace-separated query term, within an optional category; Search's title matches as you type pass none. Topic assignments still exist only for the four demo sermons, so the rest of the library has no topic labels: current counts are Spiritual Growth 3, Prayer 2, Faith 2 and Christian Living 0. The empty category is intentional.

**Every topic/tag assignment is dummy data requested for the prototype. No sermon theology or message content has been reviewed.** Each screen that shows topic labels displays the notice “Topics are sample labels, not yet checked against each sermon.”, and each row's accessible name says “Sample topics”. Verified titles are never reformatted; `SermonTitle` only draws the “|” separators in the system face so they don't read as a capital I. Do not turn `reviewStatus` into “approved” without a defined editorial review process.

Source fields remain separate from anything read from them: exact `title`, upload time, duration, thumbnail, description and tags come from YouTube; `channel` is the uploader (not a guarantee of the only speaker; guest ministers named in a title are shown in the event line). The four bundled sermons were also checked against [RCN North America](https://www.rcnnorthamerica.com/). Source spellings are preserved. Do not derive theology, Scripture citations or descriptive summaries from a title.

## Accounts, onboarding and the gate

Accounts are Supabase accounts behind an `AuthBackend` interface (`src/auth/`), so screens never import supabase-js; an on-device stand-in implements the same interface for development and tests. `useAccount()` holds the session, profile, onboarding state and the remote `guestWatch` setting; saved sermons (`useSaved()`) and watch progress (`useWatchProgress()`, `useProgressOf()`) have their own contexts. `useGate()` decides whether a guest may watch or save; when not, it stores what was tapped and opens the sign-up sheet, and `IntentRunner` carries it out once the account exists. The session is kept in the Keychain/Keystore on phones. Details, storage keys, test values and App Store notes: [accounts](accounts.md).

## Future persistence

Supabase holds accounts, profiles (display name, topics), saved sermons, watch progress, the sermon catalogue and the learning data (transcript passages keyed by `youtube_id`, needs, moments, paths and blessings), with row level security (`supabase/migrations/`). It also holds 8 AI-picked `topics` and each sermon's main topic in `sermon_topics` ([Supabase setup](../../supabase/README.md#2-create-the-tables)), which the app doesn't read yet: its topic assignments are still the local sample file, and moving them to Supabase means mapping `categories` onto those topics. Preserve stable category IDs independently of editable display labels; separate provider/video identity from an app-owned sermon ID when adding another provider. Add verified-at/source provenance and editorial review fields through an explicit migration. Only ingest explicitly approved source material.

Keep querying behind `useCatalogue()` and the list helpers so screens need not know whether content comes from the bundled sample, the device's copy or Supabase. A later ingestion process must validate unique IDs, topic references, source rights and provider availability; the current tests cover the local invariants. Watch progress is kept per account (below); a full watch history is not built.

## Media

There is **one player for the whole app** (user decision, 30 September 2026). `src/player/PlayerProvider.tsx` owns the loaded sermon and playback state (a slow "session" context and a per-second "playback" context). `src/player/PlayerHost.tsx`, rendered above the navigator in the root layout, draws the single player either in the sermon screen's Now Playing slot or docked above the tab bar; the player keeps the same place in the tree in both, so moving never reloads or restarts it. `src/player/layout.ts` gives the slot and the host the same geometry, and the sermon screen tells the host it is ready once its push transition ends.

The sermons are self-hosted (owner decision, October 2026): a pipeline uploads each one from YouTube to Cloudflare R2 as HLS (720p and 360p) at `https://audio.aaolearning.com/video/<youtubeId>/master.m3u8`. Every guided path's sermons are there and the rest are arriving. On iPhone and Android, a sermon on R2 plays in the app's own video player, `src/player/NativeVideo.tsx` (expo-video, no native controls); anything else plays in the YouTube embed. `src/player/media.ts` asks R2 whether a sermon is there (a HEAD request with a 2-second limit; a 404 until it's uploaded) and remembers yes for the session and no for ten minutes; a sermon's page asks as it opens, so Watch rarely waits. If the app's own player fails before the sermon has started, PlayerProvider hands it to YouTube for the rest of the session, from the same place; Try again (if YouTube fails too) gives the app's own player another go. If it fails while the app is away after the sermon has played (iOS can drop a paused video's decoder in the background), it loads again at the viewer's place, paused, when they come back. A speed chosen while the app's own player is paused waits for Play (on iOS setting it would start playback), and the voice keeps its pitch at any speed (`preservesPitch`, which Android's expo-video leaves off). The web keeps the YouTube embed for now (`Embed.web.tsx`; R2 allows only the `aaolearning.com` origin). Both players speak the same seam (`src/player/types.ts`): the app's commands (play, pause, seek, rate, captions) in, and YouTube's terms (ready, state, time, duration, rate, captions, error) out. The YouTube embed runs with YouTube's own controls off (`controls: 0`); `Embed.web.tsx` / `Embed.tsx` send the app's commands through the official IFrame API (`playVideo`, `pauseVideo`, `seekTo`, `setPlaybackRate` and the captions module). On a phone the embed's web view stays on the player (`src/player/youtube.ts`): YouTube's player and its frames load, the page itself never goes anywhere else, a YouTube link the viewer taps opens outside the app, and Google's sign-in and consent pages never open in it. It never opens a new window either: with `onOpenWindow` set, iOS and Android cancel them in native code and only tell the app, so no late answer can let one load. When YouTube holds a sermon back (its "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot" check, errors 150, 152 and 153, or no answer within 10 seconds of a Play sent once its player is ready; buffering gets 30 seconds, and once the sermon has played a slow restart is left to the network), the app shows its own message with **Watch on YouTube** (at the viewer's place) and **Try again**.

On the sermon screen, PlayerHost draws `src/player/stage/VideoOverlay.tsx` over the video, Mindvalley-style (owner decisions, 16 October 2026): back 15 / play-pause / forward 15 in the centre, CC at the top right, and the elapsed time, scrubber (`stage/Scrubber.tsx`), total time, speed and full screen along the bottom. While the YouTube embed plays, YouTube's title strip (the top 48pt of the video) and its logo corner (the bottom-right 96×44pt) are never covered by our buttons, shading or tap layer; over the app's own player the controls use the whole frame. While it plays, a tap on the video shows or hides the controls, and they fade 3 s after the last touch, except while VoiceOver or TalkBack is on or keyboard focus is in them on the web. While YouTube's player is paused, our shading and the full-frame tap layer step back so its pause overlay shows, and our centre play/pause, ±15 and bottom row stay. While a refused or slow start points the viewer at Play, the tap layer steps back over YouTube's player only, so its own play button works; the app's own player has no controls of its own, so there the tap layer stays and our controls can always be brought back (`tapsReachPlayer`). On a narrow frame (a 320pt phone, or large text) the speed pill moves up beside CC so the rail stays usable. Captions are off by default everywhere (`cc_load_policy: 0`, and switched off again as the player readies and first plays); CC turns them on, and its badge shows what CC asked for, off when YouTube has no track. R2 has no caption track yet, so CC is hidden while the app's own player plays (a WebVTT track is a later follow-up). Full screen on a phone turns the video 90° inside PlayerHost (the app is locked to portrait), hides the status bar and, on Android, the navigation bar (the sermon screen's `statusBarHidden` and `navigationBarHidden` options, so a swipe shows them only for a moment), keeps to the safe area, hides the page from screen readers and exits on Back or on an error; a browser turns its own page, so the web fills the window unturned and Escape exits. The video is at least 200×200 full or docked. The tab bar and dock bar measure themselves (`useChrome` in `layout.ts`), so the dock sits correctly at any text size. On a window too short for a docked video to leave 160px of page (a zoomed-in browser), the dock is a bar only: the video is hidden, paused and kept mounted, and reopening Now Playing resumes it. The scrubber uses react-native-gesture-handler (`GestureHandlerRootView` at the root) so sideways drags scrub and vertical swipes scroll the page; while dragging, its thumb follows the finger on the UI thread and only the time goes through React. Every seek the provider sends is guarded for a moment (`armSeek`), so a time polled before the player moved can't undo it and a quick ±15 counts from the new place.

Playback only starts from the viewer's tap on **Watch sermon** (or **Resume at …**), Play, a **Continue watching** tile, a path day's play (Home's path card, or **Start Day 1** / **Continue Day 3** on a path page; a sermon already in the dock plays too), or a moment in Search's results (a Watch that needed an account carries on once the account exists). Because the app's Play must be able to start the provider, the native WebView sets `mediaPlaybackRequiresUserAction={false}`; no code path plays without a tap. The app's own player keeps playing when the viewer leaves the app or locks the phone, shows the sermon on the lock screen and in Control Center (its title, "Apostle Arome Osayi" and its thumbnail) and goes into picture in picture when the viewer leaves the app while it plays (expo-video's config plugin: `supportsBackgroundPlayback`, `supportsPictureInPicture`). Leaving the app (background, not iOS "inactive") pauses a sermon playing from YouTube, and a pause while the player is still loading cancels the queued start. Closing the dock removes the player. Errors that retrying can't fix (removed, embedding disabled, unidentified player) offer Watch on YouTube instead of Try again. The app itself extracts and downloads nothing and keeps no watch history; only the last position in each sermon is kept (below).

Playback is gated for guests (see [accounts](accounts.md#the-guest-gate)): Watch asks for an account unless the sermon came from a shared link or the remote setting opens it. Third-party thumbnail/player traffic is described in the privacy screen.

The status bar is set through each screen's native-stack options (`statusBarStyle`, `statusBarHidden`, and `navigationBarHidden` for Android's navigation bar) by `useStatusBar` (`src/kit/statusBar.ts`): dark by default (the root stack's `screenOptions`), light over dark artwork (Home's hero, Paths, a cover page), hidden for a sermon in full screen, with Android's navigation bar, so both come back only for a moment after a swipe (react-native-screens sets that behaviour only when it hides the navigation bar). The app runs with `UIViewControllerBasedStatusBarAppearance` on (`app.json`), because iOS 27 ignores the old app-wide status bar calls; nothing sets the status bar any other way.

### Watch progress

For a signed-in viewer, `PlayerProvider` keeps their place in each sermon through `useWatchProgress().saveProgress` (stored in Supabase's `watch_progress`, see [accounts](accounts.md#where-data-is-kept)). It saves every 15 seconds while playing, on pause, when the player docks or closes, when another sermon loads and when the app goes to the background. A save is skipped when nothing changed, and for a sermon less than 15 seconds in that was never saved. Saves apply on screen at once and never wait on the network or hold up playback. In the background, `src/auth/watchQueue.ts` sends them:

- **Order:** one request at a time, the latest position per sermon only, so an older place never lands after a newer one.
- **Kept on the device:** each position waits in AsyncStorage for its account until the server has it, so closing the app or losing the connection loses nothing.
- **Retries:** a failed save is tried again after 2, 4, 8 … seconds (at most a minute apart), and at once when the app comes back to the foreground or the browser comes back online. Phones have no connection event without a new native module, so there the retry or the return to the app notices.
- **Saved later elsewhere:** a position that had to wait is checked against the server's list first. If another device saved the sermon later (by more than two minutes, so a phone clock a little behind can't misjudge its own place), the waiting one is dropped.
- **Offline launch:** the account's list as last shown is kept on the device too (removed when it signs out). It shows at once, Continue watching included, until the server's list arrives. Another device may have saved a newer place since, so that copy is provisional: `progressReady` stays false and nothing is saved until the server's list has been read. A sermon started meanwhile starts from the copy's place and moves to the server's when the list arrives, unless the viewer has watched on 15 seconds or chosen a place (`catchUp` in `PlayerProvider`).
- **Failure reports:** a save the server refuses is reported to Sentry ([failure alerts](failure-alerts.md)). A phone that is offline isn't.

The queue's rules are tested in Node (`tests/watch-queue.spec.ts`), and the app's offline behaviour in `tests/offline-progress.spec.ts`. Within the last 30 seconds (or the last 2% of a long sermon) a sermon counts as finished. The rules live in `src/player/progress.ts`.

`load()` starts a sermon from the saved place through the IFrame API's `start` parameter, and the Now Playing panel says **Resuming at 42:10 · Start over** until the viewer starts over (a seek to 0) or is two minutes past it. Before Watch, the poster reads **Resume at 42:10** with a progress bar along its bottom edge. Home's **Continue watching** rail (`src/home/ContinueRail.tsx`), below the path section, shows up to six unfinished sermons, most recent first, and **See all** lists every one in place; today's path sermon is left out (the path card plays it), and the rail is hidden for guests and when there's nothing to continue. A tile opens the sermon and resumes it in one tap: it leaves a short-lived request (`requestWatch` in `src/navigation.ts`) that the sermon screen carries out once it is staged, so the player never appears in the dock first. Finished sermons start from the beginning again.

## Native project

The native projects are generated (`ios/`, `android/` are git-ignored). `plugins/with-ios-scene-lifecycle.js` switches the generated iOS app to Expo's `ExpoAppSceneDelegate`, because iOS 27 won't launch an app built with its SDK without the scene life cycle; remove it once Expo's template adopts scenes. The home-screen name is "AAO" and the App Store Connect name "AAO App"; the iOS bundle identifier and Android package are `com.mshumba.aao` (user decision, 14 October 2026; permanent once the App Store Connect record exists). `userInterfaceStyle` is `light`, and the window and splash background are the ivory `#f7f4ee`. `app.config.ts` wraps `app.json` only to leave out the Sign in with Apple entitlement for free Personal Team builds (`AAO_PERSONAL_TEAM=1`). `expo-updates` is configured with the `appVersion` runtime policy ([releasing](releasing.md#over-the-air-updates-eas-update)). Until the owner's logo is added, the app icon and adaptive icon in `assets/` are a plain black square (on a black `adaptiveIcon.backgroundColor`) and the splash shows only the ivory background. `assets/icon-source/render.mjs` (`npm run icons`) still draws the earlier serif "AO" mark (Newsreader SemiBold, italic O), which the favicon still uses; running it would overwrite the black icon and the empty splash image. Its colours must change with `src/theme.ts` and `app.json`.

## Headlines and player states (30 September 2026, user decision)

Short headlines come from the catalogue (read from the title, or hand-written in `src/catalogue/editorial.ts`); `src/data/display.ts` builds the event line under them from the guest minister, event and session, leaving out what the headline already says ("Lioness Conference · Grand Finale"), then the venue, then the channel. Rows, Home, Now Playing and the dock lead with the headline; the exact title is shown under "On YouTube" on the sermon screen and is still searched. The controls on the video are back 15 / play-pause / forward 15, with the times, scrubber, speed and full screen along the bottom (see [Media](#media)). The status line under the title is always announced but shown only when it asks something of the viewer: loading, press play, still loading ("Still loading. If it doesn’t start, tap Play."), "Tap Play to start" (a browser refused the app's start; taps then also reach the player), or ended. A failure is shown where the video was, with only the actions that can work, and takes keyboard focus when it removed the focused control. Only a playing video is pinned; a poster or error scrolls with the page.
